I’ve noticed that my relationship with technology has changed quite a lot over the past few years. Like many people, I rely on my phone for work, communication, navigation, and entertainment. It’s incredibly useful, but there were times when I felt that I was checking it without any real reason.

The problem became especially clear one evening when I realised I had spent nearly an hour scrolling through social media. I hadn’t planned to do that, and I couldn’t remember most of what I had seen. It wasn’t a disaster, of course, but it made me wonder how often I was giving my attention to things I didn’t actually care about.

I decided to make a few changes rather than delete all my apps. First, I turned off notifications that weren’t important. Then I moved social media apps away from my home screen and started leaving my phone in another room while I was working. In the evenings, I tried to read or go for a walk before checking messages.

The results were not immediate. I still reached for my phone out of habit, especially when I was bored or waiting for something. However, after a few weeks, I became more aware of those moments and found it easier to choose what I wanted to do.

I don’t think technology itself is the problem. The more useful question is whether we’re using it in ways that support our priorities. For me, the goal is not to spend as little time online as possible, but to make that time feel more intentional.
