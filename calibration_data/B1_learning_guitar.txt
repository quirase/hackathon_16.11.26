A few years ago, I decided to learn how to play the guitar. I had always enjoyed listening to music, but I had never learned to play an instrument. One evening, I saw a video of someone playing a song I liked, and I thought it would be interesting to try it myself.

At first, I borrowed an old guitar from a friend. I found some free lessons online and started practising for about twenty minutes every day. The first few weeks were difficult. My fingers hurt, and I couldn’t change between chords quickly enough. Sometimes I felt disappointed because the songs sounded very different from the original recordings.

After a while, I understood that I was expecting too much too soon. I began choosing simpler songs and paying attention to small improvements. I also joined a group of beginners who met once a week. Playing with other people was a little uncomfortable at first, but everyone was supportive, and we often laughed at our mistakes.

Now I can play several songs from beginning to end. I’m still not an expert, and I don’t practise every day, but playing the guitar has become a relaxing part of my routine. The experience taught me that learning a new skill takes patience, and that progress is easier to notice when you look back after a few months.
